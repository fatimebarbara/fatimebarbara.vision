The Future of Cities.

A Data-Driven Approach to Tackling Grand Challenges and Building Sustainable, Resilient Communities.

Cities face many challenges at once, and that complexity is also where change becomes possible. The book looks at how mayors and other place leaders can use data and citizen science to build the cities we actually want to live in.

About the book

Our cities, our future. Cities are at a crossroads.

Between data and dreams, we find the courage to reimagine our cities — and ourselves.

The Future of Cities explores how place leaders armed with data-driven decision-making and citizen science can tackle the grand challenges of our time: sustainability, health, education, security and poverty.

Using real-world examples and data analysis, the book shows how data-driven leadership helps cities find solutions that work for local communities, with practical steps towards a more sustainable and fairer future.

Key insights

01

Using data in decision-making

To tackle challenges linked to sustainability, water, the environment, public transportation, health, education, poverty and safety.

02

Real-world examples and actionable steps

For building equitable, resilient cities, drawn from European urban contexts and global comparisons.

03

Citizen science as a tool

To build collaboration and collective intelligence in urban planning, turning residents from subjects of policy into co-authors of it.

European Parliament

A full hearing at the European Parliament.

The book was presented at a dedicated session at the European Parliament in Brussels in October 2025: a discussion among MEPs, EU institutional leaders and policy heads on the future of European cities and the data-driven approaches that can shape them.

“The future belongs to the cities that can adapt.”

The session

The session was hosted by Gabriella Gerzsenyi MEP, Vice-Chair of the REGI Committee, and opened by Jan Olbrycht, former EPP Group MEP and Chairman of the former Urban Intergroup.

Following the book presentation, a panel discussion brought together MEPs, EU institutional leaders and policy heads from across the urban policy landscape, including representatives from the European Commission’s DG REGIO, the EPP Group, Eurocities and the EPP Committee of Regions Group.

The discussion was about the future of European cities and how evidence can guide how they develop over the next decade.

About the author

An innovation researcher and an artist.

Dr. Fatime Barbara Hegyi is an innovation economy researcher and artist, working with data analysis and citizen science to help urban leaders find solutions that work. As both scientist and artist, she is driven by curiosity and by building tools and methods for a fairer, more sustainable world.

At the Joint Research Centre of the European Commission, her work supported national, regional and urban governments in policy development across diverse fields, including research and innovation. The tools and frameworks she has developed for assessing urban innovation districts, monitoring and evaluating policy processes and enhancing public leadership are widely published, including works by Palgrave Macmillan and Edward Elgar Publishing.

She speaks regularly at international and academic events. Her interest in public leadership led her to write this book, drawing on years of research into how leadership drives change in cities. She also painted the book’s cover artwork.
